Looking for an OurHome alternative?

By the maker of Pocket Piggy · Published October 10, 2026 · Updated October 10, 2026

If your family used the OurHome chores app and can't find it anymore, you're not imagining it. Here is what we found on the US App Store, the two newer apps that now use the OurHome name, and how Pocket Piggy compares for chores and allowance, including where it falls short.

Full disclosure: we make Pocket Piggy. Every claim about another app on this page comes only from its US App Store listing, with the date we read it. We couldn't find an official statement from the original OurHome developer, so we don't guess at what happened to it.

Where did OurHome go?

When we searched the US App Store for "OurHome" on October 10, 2026, the original family chores app did not appear. The two apps that came up with OurHome at the front of their names are newer apps from different developers: OurHome by Elusios, first released in October 2025, and OurHome: Family Planner, first released in September 2026. (as of October 10, 2026; sources: OurHome by Elusios, US App Store listing; OurHome: Family Planner, US App Store listing)

If the old app still opens on a family device, write down each kid's balance before you switch, so you can carry it over to whatever you use next.

The two apps that use the name today

Neither is the original app, but you may land on them while searching. These details come from each app's US App Store listing. Prices and features change, so check the linked listing before you decide.

OurHome by Elusios

  • Describes itself as a household assistant for managing a home with roommates: shopping lists, tasks organized by room and assigned to specific people, recurring tasks with deadlines, and a built-in calendar. (as of October 10, 2026; source: OurHome by Elusios, US App Store listing)
  • Free to download, with one in-app purchase listed: OurHome Premium, $1.99. The listing doesn't say whether that is a one-time purchase or a subscription. (as of October 10, 2026; source: OurHome by Elusios, US App Store listing)
  • The version history lists rewards that can be added and managed, and offline members such as kids who need no email address, both as Premium features. (as of October 10, 2026; source: OurHome by Elusios, US App Store listing, version history)
  • Compatibility lists iPhone, iPad, iPod touch, Mac and Apple Vision. Apple TV is not listed. (as of October 10, 2026; source: OurHome by Elusios, US App Store listing)

OurHome: Family Planner

  • Describes itself as a shared space for couples and families: a shared calendar, shared lists for groceries, to-dos and shopping, and a feed of household activity. (as of October 10, 2026; source: OurHome: Family Planner, US App Store listing)
  • Free to download with a 14-day free trial, and in-app purchases: Family Monthly $8.99, Family Yearly $69.99, Couples Monthly $4.99 and Couples Yearly $39.99. The Family plan covers up to 6 members and the Couples plan up to 2. (as of October 10, 2026; source: OurHome: Family Planner, US App Store listing)
  • Compatibility lists iPhone, iPod touch, Mac and Apple Vision. iPad and Apple TV are not listed. (as of October 10, 2026; source: OurHome: Family Planner, US App Store listing)
  • The listing does not mention allowance, rewards or kids' chore checklists. (as of October 10, 2026; source: OurHome: Family Planner, US App Store listing)

Where another app may be a better fit

  • You share a home with roommates, not kids. OurHome by Elusios is built around tasks assigned to roommates, by room. (as of October 10, 2026; source: OurHome by Elusios, US App Store listing) Pocket Piggy is built for families with kids.
  • You mostly want a shared calendar and grocery list. Both newer OurHome apps list a shared calendar and shared lists. (as of October 10, 2026; sources: OurHome by Elusios; OurHome: Family Planner, US App Store listings) Pocket Piggy is a chore and allowance app, not a family calendar.
  • Someone in the family uses Android. Pocket Piggy is Apple only. Our roundup of chore and allowance apps covers options that list Android.

Where Pocket Piggy may be a better fit

  • Chores that pay. Kids check off chores, a parent approves, and the money adds up for Pay Day on a day you pick, with a countdown and a celebration. If a chore from a week that already paid out is approved late, that Pay Day adjusts automatically. See the allowance features.
  • Spend, Save and Give. Earnings split automatically into three jars, with savings goals, giving to a named recipient and optional parent-paid interest on savings.
  • No subscription. Pocket Piggy is free for one kid, and the Family Unlock is $9.99 once. That adds more kids and lets you invite another grown-up on their own Apple Account. How pricing works.
  • A family board on Apple TV. The whole week on the living room screen: one row per person, each day's chores, balances and a Pay Day countdown. It is view-only, and the Apple TV app is free. See the Apple TV board.
  • Chores that take turns. A chore can belong to one person, rotate weekly or monthly between several people (grown-ups included), or be up for grabs for whoever gets to it first. See the chore chart.
  • Read-aloud for kids who are still learning to read. Kid Mode has speaker buttons that read the screen and each chore aloud.
  • No accounts for kids. Kids need no account, email or sign-up, and there is no Pocket Piggy account for anyone. Family data stays on your devices and in your own iCloud.

Where Pocket Piggy falls short

We would rather you find out here than after you download it:

  • No Android. Pocket Piggy runs only on Apple devices: iPhone, iPad, Apple TV and a view-only Apple Watch app. If anyone in the family uses Android, it won't work for them.
  • No required photo proof. A kid can add a photo when they check off a chore, but you can't make a photo required before a chore counts. Every chore does need a parent's approval before money moves.
  • No real money. There is no debit card, no bank account and no way to send money. Pocket Piggy keeps track of what you owe each kid, and you pay them however you already do.
  • Money only, no points. Rewards are tracked as money, not points or prizes.
  • Widgets and the Watch are view-only. Kids check off chores in Kid Mode on an iPhone or iPad, behind a parent's PIN.

Choosing for your family

If what you miss most is a shared family calendar and grocery list, one of the newer OurHome apps or a family organizer may suit you better. If you used OurHome so kids could check off chores and earn for them, and your family is all Apple, Pocket Piggy may be the closer fit, with allowance, Spend, Save and Give and a board on the TV built in. It's free for one kid, so you can try it without paying anything. For more options, read the best chore and allowance apps for 2026, or compare prices across several chore apps.
