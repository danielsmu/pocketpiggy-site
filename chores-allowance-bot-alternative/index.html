Pocket Piggy and Chores & Allowance Bot: how they compare

By the maker of Pocket Piggy · Published October 3, 2026 · Updated October 3, 2026

Chores & Allowance Bot and Pocket Piggy both track chores and allowance without moving real money. Here is an honest look at how they differ, with every Chores & Allowance Bot detail linked to its official source, and a plain list of where Pocket Piggy falls short.

Free for one kid · $9.99 once for the whole family · no subscription

Full disclosure: we make Pocket Piggy. Every claim about Chores & Allowance Bot on this page comes only from its US App Store listing, with the date we read it. Chores & Allowance Bot has a long feature list, and for some families it will be the better choice. We say where.

Two apps with a lot in common

Both apps keep a running record of what each kid has earned, with chores that need a parent's approval, rotating chores, up-for-grabs chores and read-aloud for kids who are still learning to read. In both, the balance is what the family owes the kid, and you hand over the money yourself. Chores & Allowance Bot lets you Deduct money from children accounts when they spend money. (as of October 3, 2026; source: Chores & Allowance Bot, US App Store listing) Pocket Piggy works the same way: no bank account or card is connected, and you pay kids in cash, by bank transfer or however you already do.

The biggest differences are how you pay for the app, which devices it runs on, how much you can fine-tune, and where your family's data lives.

Where Chores & Allowance Bot may be a better fit

  • You want a photo before a chore counts. An option can require a photo for each completed chore. (as of October 3, 2026; source: Chores & Allowance Bot, US App Store listing)
  • You want the app in another language. The listing names eleven languages, including Spanish, French, Hindi, Japanese and Simplified Chinese. (as of October 3, 2026; source: Chores & Allowance Bot, US App Store listing)
  • You want chores to rotate every day. Rotation can be daily, weekly or monthly. (as of October 3, 2026; source: Chores & Allowance Bot, US App Store listing)
  • Each kid has their own passcode. Optional child passcodes keep each child's chore list private from siblings. (as of October 3, 2026; source: Chores & Allowance Bot, US App Store listing)
  • You'd rather use points. Pretend currencies such as Points and Smiley Faces are supported. (as of October 3, 2026; source: Chores & Allowance Bot, US App Store listing)
  • You like fine-grained allowance rules. Partial allowance based on the difficulty of chores done, automatic approval based on required chores, and as many accounts and goals per child as you want. (as of October 3, 2026; source: Chores & Allowance Bot, US App Store listing)
  • You use a Mac. Compatibility lists Mac. (as of October 3, 2026; source: Chores & Allowance Bot, US App Store listing)

Where Pocket Piggy may be a better fit

  • No subscription. Pocket Piggy is free for one kid, and the Family Unlock is $9.99 once. That adds more kids and lets you invite another grown-up on their own Apple Account. How pricing works.
  • A family board on Apple TV. The whole week on the living room screen: one row per person, each day's chores, balances and a Pay Day countdown. It is view-only, and the Apple TV app is free. See the Apple TV board.
  • Spend, Save and Give. Earnings split automatically into three jars, with savings goals, giving to a named recipient and optional parent-paid interest on savings. See the allowance features.
  • Pay Day. Money moves on a day you pick, with a countdown and a celebration. If a chore from a week that already paid out is approved late, that Pay Day adjusts automatically.
  • Grown-ups in the rotation. Parents can be added to the family to take turns on chores, without an allowance of their own.
  • No accounts, and your data stays in your iCloud. Kids need no account, email or sign-up, and there is no Pocket Piggy account for anyone. Family data is stored on your devices and in your own iCloud, and no Pocket Piggy server receives it. The app sends anonymous usage analytics with no kid names or chore titles. See the privacy policy.
  • A printable chore chart for the fridge, as a monthly family sheet or a weekly tick sheet per person.

Where Pocket Piggy falls short

We would rather you find out here than after you download it:

  • No Android. Pocket Piggy runs only on Apple devices: iPhone, iPad, Apple TV and a view-only Apple Watch app. If anyone in the family uses Android, it won't work for them.
  • No required photo proof. A kid can add a photo when they check off a chore, but you can't make a photo required before a chore counts. Every chore does need a parent's approval before money moves.
  • No real money. There is no debit card, no bank account and no way to send money. Pocket Piggy keeps track of what you owe each kid, and you pay them however you already do.
  • Weekly or monthly rotation only. Chores can't rotate every day.
  • No per-kid passcodes and no points. Kid Mode uses one parent PIN, and balances are always money.
  • Widgets and the Watch are view-only. Kids check off chores in Kid Mode on an iPhone or iPad, behind a parent's PIN.

Choosing for your family

If you want photo proof before a chore counts, daily rotation, a passcode for each kid or the app in another language, Chores & Allowance Bot is likely the better pick. If your family is all Apple, you'd rather not pay for another subscription, and you'd like the week's chores on the TV with Spend, Save and Give built in, Pocket Piggy may suit you. It's free for one kid, so you can try it without paying anything. For more options, read the best chore and allowance apps for 2026, or compare prices across several chore apps.
