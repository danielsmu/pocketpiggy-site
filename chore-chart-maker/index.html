    Getting the most from a chore chart

    A chore chart works well when it's simple, visible and checked often. Start with fewer chores than you think, around three to five a day, and make sure each one has a clear finish line. "Put your shoes on the rack" is easier to get right than "keep the hallway tidy."

    Let your child help build the chart. Kids who choose some of their own chores, or decide the order, tend to stick with them longer. For younger kids, a quick drawing next to each chore helps them read the chart on their own.

    Pick a regular time to look at it together. After dinner or before bed works for most families. That's the moment to tick boxes, notice effort, and fix anything that isn't working. If a chore keeps getting skipped, it may be at the wrong time of day, or it may just be too big. Split it up or swap it.

    If chores earn money, add it up on the Pay Day row at the end of the week and count it out together. Seeing the ticks turn into money makes the connection real. Our allowance calculator can help you plan the numbers, and there are ready-made printable charts by age if you'd rather start from one of those.
