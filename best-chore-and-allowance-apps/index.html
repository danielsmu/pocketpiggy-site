How we chose

We looked for apps a US family can download from the App Store today that help with chores, allowance, or both. For each one we read its US App Store listing (the price list under In-App Purchases and the Compatibility section) or, for the card apps, its official website. We did not use review sites, and we do not show star ratings. We describe what each app says it does; we did not test every feature ourselves.

1. Pocket Piggy: best if you want to pay once and see chores on the TV

Pocket Piggy is a chore chart and allowance tracker for iPhone, iPad and Apple TV. Kids check off chores in a PIN-protected Kid Mode, a parent approves, and money is added to the kid's Spend, Save and Give jars on Pay Day. Chores can belong to one person, take turns weekly or monthly, or be up for grabs for whoever gets there first. Kid Mode can read the screen aloud for kids who are still learning to read. The family board shows on Apple TV, and kids never need an account or email.

  • Price: free for one kid. A one-time $9.99 Family Unlock adds more kids and lets you invite another grown-up on their own Apple Account. No subscription. How pricing works.
  • Devices: iPhone, iPad and Apple TV, plus widgets and a view-only Apple Watch app.

Where it falls short. We would rather you find out here than after you download it:

  • No Android. Pocket Piggy is only for Apple devices. If anyone in the family uses Android, it won't work for them.
  • No required photo proof. A kid can add a photo when they check off a chore, but you can't make a photo required before a chore counts.
  • No real money. There is no debit card, no bank account and no way to send money. Pocket Piggy keeps track of what you owe each kid, and you pay them in cash, by bank transfer or however you already do.
  • Weekly or monthly rotation only. Chores can't rotate every day.

2. Chorsee: best for photo proof

  • Chores can be assigned to one or more people, rotate between family members, or be up for grabs for whoever gets to them first. (as of September 26, 2026; source: Chorsee, US App Store listing)
  • Photo proof can be required for specific chores. (as of September 26, 2026; source: Chorsee, US App Store listing)
  • Kids' devices can be limited to their own chore list, without editing chores or balances, and home screen and lock screen widgets can mark off chores. (as of September 26, 2026; source: Chorsee, US App Store listing)
  • Rewards can be allowance, points or no rewards at all. (as of September 26, 2026; source: Chorsee, US App Store listing)
  • Free to download, with in-app purchases including Chorsee Monthly $8.99, Chorsee Yearly $39.99 and Chorsee Lifetime $119.99. The listing also shows a $89.99 yearly and a $224.99 lifetime option. (as of September 26, 2026; source: Chorsee, US App Store listing)
  • Compatibility lists iPhone, iPad, iPod touch, Mac and Apple Vision. (as of September 26, 2026; source: Chorsee, US App Store listing)

May suit you if you want to see a photo before you approve a chore, or you want a lifetime option and a Mac app. See how Pocket Piggy and Chorsee compare.

3. Chores & Allowance Bot: best for pre-readers and other languages

  • Chores can rotate between children daily, weekly or monthly, and up-for-grabs chores can be assigned to several children but done by only one. (as of September 26, 2026; source: Chores & Allowance Bot, US App Store listing)
  • Pre-readers can have chore names, descriptions, allowance and savings amounts read aloud. (as of September 26, 2026; source: Chores & Allowance Bot, US App Store listing)
  • A photo can be required for each completed chore. (as of September 26, 2026; source: Chores & Allowance Bot, US App Store listing)
  • A percentage of allowance and chore payments can be moved automatically into separate accounts and goals. (as of September 26, 2026; source: Chores & Allowance Bot, US App Store listing)
  • Languages listed: English, French, German, Hindi, Italian, Japanese, Korean, Portuguese, Russian, Simplified Chinese and Spanish. (as of September 26, 2026; source: Chores & Allowance Bot, US App Store listing)
  • Free to download, with in-app purchases Premium Monthly $9.99, Premium Semiannual $17.99 and Premium Annual $39.99. (as of September 26, 2026; source: Chores & Allowance Bot, US App Store listing)
  • Compatibility lists iPhone, iPad, iPod touch, Mac and Apple Vision. (as of September 26, 2026; source: Chores & Allowance Bot, US App Store listing)

May suit you if your family uses one of those languages, or you want chores to rotate every day. See how Pocket Piggy and Chores & Allowance Bot compare.

4. Homey: best for paying allowance into a bank account

  • Chores can recur daily, weekly or monthly or happen once, and you can separate unpaid responsibilities from jobs that earn money. (as of September 26, 2026; source: Homey, US App Store listing)
  • Allowance can be transferred to a bank account (US only) or marked as paid out in cash. (as of September 26, 2026; source: Homey, US App Store listing)
  • Kids can have saving jars with financial goals. (as of September 26, 2026; source: Homey, US App Store listing)
  • Free to download, with in-app purchases including Homey Monthly $6.99 and Homey Yearly $59.99. (as of September 26, 2026; source: Homey, US App Store listing)
  • Compatibility lists iPhone, iPad, iPod touch, Mac and Apple Vision. (as of September 26, 2026; source: Homey, US App Store listing)

May suit you if you want allowance to land in a real bank account without a separate card app.

5. S'moresUp: best for chores plus a family calendar

  • Kids earn S'mores (points) for chores and actions, with allowance and money lessons built around them. (as of September 26, 2026; source: S'moresUp, US App Store listing)
  • A collaborative family planner for appointments and events, and "Family Campfires" for keeping in touch with extended family. (as of September 26, 2026; source: S'moresUp, US App Store listing)
  • Free to download, with in-app purchases Monthly $7.99 and Yearly $79.99. (as of September 26, 2026; source: S'moresUp, US App Store listing)
  • Designed for iPhone; compatibility lists iPhone, iPod touch and Mac. There is no iPad version listed. (as of September 26, 2026; source: S'moresUp, US App Store listing)

May suit you if you want one app for the family schedule as well as chores, and everyone uses an iPhone.

6. Greenlight: best for a real debit card

  • Four plans: Core $5.99/month, Max $10.98/month, Infinity $15.98/month and Family Shield $19.98/month. (as of September 26, 2026; source: Greenlight plans)
  • Every plan covers up to 5 kids and includes debit cards that parents manage. (as of September 26, 2026; source: Greenlight plans)

May suit you if the main goal is giving kids real spending money on a card. Greenlight is a different kind of product from a chore tracker. See how Pocket Piggy and Greenlight compare.

7. BusyKid: best for a spend card plus investing

  • $4 a month, billed annually ($48 a year) for the whole family. (as of September 26, 2026; source: BusyKid features and pricing)
  • Every subscription comes with up to five BusyKid Visa prepaid cards for kids ages 5 to 17. (as of September 26, 2026; source: BusyKid features and pricing)
  • Preset chores and allowances by age that parents can change, automatic allowance allocations, and the option to pay allowance separately from chores. (as of September 26, 2026; source: BusyKid features and pricing)
  • Investing in stocks and ETFs from the Save area of the app, with no commission to buy or sell. (as of September 26, 2026; source: BusyKid features and pricing)

May suit you if you want chores, a real card and investing in one place. See how Pocket Piggy and BusyKid compare.

Which one should you pick?

  • You want kids to hold real money on a card: Greenlight or BusyKid. Pocket Piggy can't do this.
  • Someone in the family uses Android: pick an app that runs on their phone. Pocket Piggy is Apple only.
  • You want to see a photo before approving a chore: Chorsee or Chores & Allowance Bot.
  • You want to avoid another subscription, or see the week on your TV: Pocket Piggy. It is free for one kid, so you can try it before paying anything.
