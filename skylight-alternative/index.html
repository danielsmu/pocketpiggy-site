Pocket Piggy and Skylight: how they compare for family chores

By the maker of Pocket Piggy · Published October 7, 2026 · Updated October 7, 2026

Skylight and Pocket Piggy both put the family's chores somewhere everyone can see them. Skylight does it with its own calendar display. Pocket Piggy does it on an Apple TV you may already own. Here is an honest look at how they differ, with every Skylight detail linked to its official source, and a plain list of where Pocket Piggy falls short.

Download Pocket Piggy

Free for one kid · $9.99 once for the whole family · no subscription

Full disclosure: we make Pocket Piggy. Every claim about Skylight on this page comes only from the Skylight App's US App Store listing, with the date we read it. Skylight is a family organizer that does a lot more than chores, and for many families it will be the better choice. We say where.

Two different ideas of a family board

Skylight describes itself as the operating system for your family, bringing everyone's calendars, lists, routines, and memories into one place. Its app can be used to activate and manage your Skylight Calendar and Skylight Frame. (as of October 7, 2026; source: Skylight App, US App Store listing) Chores are one part of a bigger organizer that also covers calendars, lists, meal plans and photos.

Pocket Piggy does less, on purpose. It is a chore and allowance app: chores that take turns, a parent's approval for every chore, and money that adds up toward Pay Day in Spend, Save and Give jars. The family board shows on an Apple TV, so there is no dedicated display to buy if you already have one. It does not do calendars, lists or meal plans.

So the real question is what you want the screen on the wall or under the TV to do. If it's the whole family's schedule, Skylight is built for that. If it's chores and what kids have earned, read on.

Where Skylight may be a better fit

  • You want one place for the whole family's schedule. Skylight syncs calendars and lets you create events, alongside chores and routines. (as of October 7, 2026; source: Skylight App, US App Store listing) Pocket Piggy has no calendar.
  • Shared lists and meal planning. Skylight has grocery and to-do lists, plus a recipe book and meal plans with Plus. (as of October 7, 2026; source: Skylight App, US App Store listing)
  • You don't have an Apple TV, or the TV isn't where the family gathers. Skylight's board lives on its own display. Pocket Piggy's board needs an Apple TV signed in to your Apple Account.
  • Stars and rewards instead of money. With Plus, kids earn stars and unlock Rewards for completed tasks. (as of October 7, 2026; source: Skylight App, US App Store listing) Pocket Piggy only tracks money.
  • Family photos on the same screen. With Plus, photos and videos can be used as screensavers. (as of October 7, 2026; source: Skylight App, US App Store listing)
  • You use a Mac. The Skylight listing includes Mac compatibility. (as of October 7, 2026; source: Skylight App, US App Store listing)

Where Pocket Piggy may be a better fit

  • No device to buy, if you have an Apple TV. The whole week on the living room screen: one row per person, each day's chores, balances and a Pay Day countdown. The Apple TV app is free. See the Apple TV board.
  • No subscription. Pocket Piggy is free for one kid, and the Family Unlock is $9.99 once. That adds more kids and lets you invite another grown-up on their own Apple Account. How pricing works.
  • Chores that pay. Weekly or monthly allowance, or pay per chore, with Spend, Save and Give jars, savings goals, giving to a named recipient and optional parent-paid interest on savings. See the allowance features.
  • Fair turns. Chores can rotate between people each week or month, or be up for grabs for whoever gets there first. Grown-ups can take turns too.
  • A parent says yes before money moves. Every chore waits for a grown-up's approval. If a chore from a week that already paid out is approved late, that Pay Day adjusts automatically.
  • Read-aloud for kids who are still learning to read. Kid Mode has speaker buttons that read the screen and each chore aloud.
  • No accounts for kids. Kids need no account, email or sign-up, and there is no Pocket Piggy account for anyone. Family data stays on your devices and in your own iCloud.
  • A printable chore chart for the fridge, as a monthly family sheet or a weekly tick sheet per person.

Where Pocket Piggy falls short

We would rather you find out here than after you download it:

  • No family calendar, lists or meal planning. Pocket Piggy is only for chores and allowance. If you want the family's schedule on the wall, it won't replace that.
  • The TV board is view-only. Nobody can check off or approve chores on the TV. Kids check off chores in Kid Mode on an iPhone or iPad, behind a parent's PIN.
  • The TV needs your Apple Account. The Apple TV must be signed in to the same Apple Account as the parent's iPhone or iPad. There is no way to show a family from a different Apple Account on it. And if you don't own an Apple TV, that's a device to buy too.
  • No Android. Pocket Piggy runs only on Apple devices: iPhone, iPad, Apple TV and a view-only Apple Watch app. If anyone in the family uses Android, it won't work for them.
  • No required photo proof. A kid can add a photo when they check off a chore, but you can't make a photo required before a chore counts.
  • No real money. There is no debit card, no bank account and no way to send money. Pocket Piggy keeps track of what you owe each kid, and you pay them however you already do.
  • Weekly or monthly rotation only. Chores can't rotate every day.

Choosing for your family

If you want the family calendar, shopping lists, meal plans and chores together on a dedicated screen, Skylight is likely the better pick. If what you really need is chores that take turns, a parent's OK on each one, and an allowance kids can watch grow, on a TV you already have and without another subscription, Pocket Piggy may suit you. It's free for one kid, and the Apple TV app has a sample family you can explore before setting anything up.
